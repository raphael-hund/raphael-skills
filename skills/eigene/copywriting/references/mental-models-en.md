## Understanding Buyers & Human Psychology

**Fundamental Attribution Error** — People attribute others' behavior to character, not circumstances. *Application:* when customers don't convert, examine your process before blaming them.

**Mere Exposure Effect** — People prefer things they've seen before. *Application:* consistent brand presence builds preference over time.

**Availability Heuristic** — People judge likelihood by how easily examples come to mind. *Application:* case studies/testimonials make success feel achievable.

**Confirmation Bias** — People seek confirming information, ignore contradicting evidence. *Application:* align messaging with what the audience already believes; don't fight beliefs head-on.

**Mimetic Desire** — People want things because others want them. *Application:* waitlists, exclusivity, social proof trigger mimetic desire.

**Sunk Cost Fallacy** — People keep investing because of past investment. *Application:* know when to kill underperforming campaigns.

**Endowment Effect** — People value things more once they own them. *Application:* free trials/samples let customers "own" the product.

**IKEA Effect** — People value things more when they've put effort in. *Application:* let customers customize/configure something.

**Zero-Price Effect** — "Free" triggers disproportionate, irrational preference. *Application:* free tiers/trials/shipping have outsized appeal.

**Hyperbolic Discounting / Present Bias** — People prefer immediate rewards over future ones. *Application:* emphasize immediate benefits over future ones.

**Status-Quo Bias** — People prefer the current state; change feels risky. *Application:* reduce switching friction, make transition feel safe.

**Default Effect** — People accept pre-selected options. *Application:* pre-select the plan you want chosen.

**Paradox of Choice** — Too many options overwhelm. *Application:* three pricing tiers beat seven; recommend one "best for most".

**Goal-Gradient Effect** — People accelerate as they approach a goal. *Application:* progress bars, "almost there" messaging.

**Peak-End Rule** — People judge experiences by the peak and the end, not the average. *Application:* design memorable peaks and strong endings (thank-you pages, follow-ups).

**Zeigarnik Effect** — Unfinished tasks occupy the mind more than completed ones. *Application:* "You're 80% done" creates pull to finish.

**Pratfall Effect** — Competent people become more likable after a small admitted flaw. *Application:* "We're not the cheapest, but..." can increase trust.

**Curse of Knowledge** — Experts struggle to explain simply, once they know something. *Application:* test copy with people unfamiliar with your space.

**Mental Accounting** — People treat money differently by source/intended use, even though it's fungible. *Application:* "$3/day" feels different from "$90/month" — same number, different frame.

**Regret Aversion** — People avoid actions that might cause regret. *Application:* money-back guarantees, free trials, "no commitment" reduce regret fear.

**Bandwagon Effect / Social Proof** — People follow what others do. *Application:* customer counts, testimonials, logos, "trending" indicators.

## Influencing Behavior & Persuasion

**Reciprocity Principle** — People feel obligated to return favors. *Application:* give value (free content/tools) before asking for anything.

**Commitment & Consistency** — Once committed, people want to stay consistent. *Application:* get small commitments first (email signup, trial) before bigger asks.

**Authority Bias** — People defer to experts/authority. *Application:* expert endorsements, certifications, "featured in" logos.

**Liking / Similarity Bias** — People say yes to those they like/resemble. *Application:* relatable spokespeople, founder stories, community language.

**Unity Principle** — Shared identity drives influence. *Application:* position the brand as part of the customer's tribe, insider language.

**Scarcity / Urgency Heuristic** — Limited availability raises perceived value. *Application:* limited-time offers, low-stock warnings — only when genuine (see Gotchas in SKILL.md).

**Foot-in-the-Door** — Small request first, then escalate. *Application:* trial → paid plan → annual → enterprise.

**Door-in-the-Face** — Unreasonably large request first, then retreat to the real ask. *Application:* show enterprise pricing first, then reveal the affordable plan.

**Loss Aversion / Prospect Theory** — Losses feel ~2x more painful than equivalent gains feel good. *Application:* "Don't miss out" beats "You could gain."

**Anchoring Effect** — The first number seen heavily influences subsequent judgments. *Application:* show the higher price first (original/competitor/enterprise tier) to anchor.

**Decoy Effect** — A third, inferior option makes one of the original two look better. *Application:* a deliberately weak pricing tier steers toward the preferred one.

**Framing Effect** — Presentation changes perception of identical facts. *Application:* "90% success rate" vs. "10% failure rate" — frame positively.

**Contrast Effect** — Things seem different depending on comparison. *Application:* show the "before" state clearly so "after" feels vivid.

## Pricing Psychology

**Charm Pricing / Left-Digit Effect** — Prices ending in 9 seem much lower ($99 vs $100). *Application:* .99/.95 endings for value-focused products.

**Rounded-Price (Fluency) Effect** — Round numbers feel premium, easier to process. *Application:* round prices for premium products, charm prices for value products.

**Rule of 100** — Under $100: percentage discounts look bigger ("20% off"). Over $100: absolute discounts look bigger ("$50 off"). *Application:* pick the framing that yields the bigger-looking number.

**Price Relativity / Good-Better-Best** — People judge price relative to the options shown. *Application:* three tiers, middle is the target; the expensive tier makes the middle look reasonable.

**Mental Accounting (Pricing)** — Same price, different frame changes perception. *Application:* "$1/day" feels cheaper than "$30/month."
